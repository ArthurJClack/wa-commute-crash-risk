The estimate multiplies a Washington baseline crash rate by factors for your route and for you, then converts the expected number of crashes over your miles into a probability: P = 1 − e−rate × miles.

Baseline (Washington)

  • 1.75 police-reported collisions per million vehicle miles (WSDOT, 2023), × 1.8 vehicles per collision ≈ 3.15 crash involvements per million miles for a typical driver.
  • About 30% of involvements include an injury.
  • Fatal crash involvement ≈ 1.67 per 100 million miles (FARS 2024: 674 fatal crashes, 60.65 billion miles driven, ~1.5 vehicles each).

Route factors

  • Road type mix. Each stretch of the route is classed from the routing engine as interstate, highway, arterial or local street. Freeways have fewer crashes per mile driven; arterials have the most; rural highways have the highest share of deaths.
  • Local fatal-crash history. Every FARS crash within 100 m of the route in the years loaded is counted, then compared with how many you'd expect on that road mix given typical Washington traffic volumes. The ratio is shrunk toward 1 so a single crash can't swing the result, and capped between 0.6× and 2.5×.

Personal factors

  • Age: AAA Foundation crash involvement per mile driven by age group.
  • Time of day: night driving roughly triples fatal crash risk per mile (NHTSA); rush hours raise fender-benders.
  • Weather: rain raises crash rates about 70% and snow/ice about 85% (Qiu & Nixon meta-analysis).
  • Phone: handheld use roughly 2–4× crash risk (Virginia Tech naturalistic driving study).
  • Speed, vehicle and seat belt: speeding raises both crash odds and severity; motorcycles raise fatal risk about 13× per mile; going unbelted raises the chance a crash kills or injures you.

Limits

This is a statistical estimate for a typical driver with your characteristics, not a prediction. The crash map only contains fatal crashes, because Washington's full crash database is not published as a public API. Traffic volumes per road type are typical values, not measured counts on your exact roads. Treat the grade as a comparison tool: try different departure times or alternate routes and see which is safer.
